• Premenstrual Phase (Leading up to period start, roughly Day -14 to Day -1/0):
    ◦ This phase is most commonly associated with negative mood.
    ◦ For happy–sad mood, there is a large decrease in happiness observed before the period begins. The amplitude of this menstrual cycle effect on happy–sad mood is 5.5%, which translates to a 22% relative change in the probability of logging sadness.
    ◦ The premenstrual negative mood effect increases with age, specifically from 3.6% in 15–20-year-olds to 5.4% in 30–35-year-olds, representing a relative increase of 51%.
    ◦ This premenstrual decrease in happiness is directionally consistent across all 87 countries examined in the study.
    ◦ Other mood dimensions where the menstrual cycle is the largest in amplitude, suggesting a negative shift during this phase, include: happy–sensitive (likely more sensitive), energized–exhausted (likely more exhausted), focused–distracted (likely more distracted), motivated–unmotivated (likely less motivated), sociable–withdrawn (likely more withdrawn), and supportive social–conflict social (likely more conflict).

• Period Start (Day 0):
    ◦ Moods, particularly happiness, are likely at a low point around the start of the period, as the decline occurs in the premenstrual phase.

• Post-Period / Follicular & Ovulatory Phases (After period starts, roughly Day 1 onwards):
    ◦ Following the premenstrual decline, happiness tends to rise again after the period begins.
    ◦ As the premenstrual phase is the most commonly associated with negative mood, these subsequent phases generally exhibit more positive or "less negative" states for the aforementioned mood dimensions (e.g., happier, less sensitive, more energized, more focused, more motivated, more sociable, and more supportive in social interactions).
